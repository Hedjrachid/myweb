Learning useful structure from complex data.

3. Research and scholarship

3.1 Research vision and development

3.1.1 A continuing question across different applications

The central question in my research is how to learn useful and interpretable structure from complex data. My early work addressed this through image segmentation, document restoration, and multispectral analysis; later work extended it to remote sensing, matrix factorization, clustering, manifold learning, and medical data analysis. My recent work on meta-unsupervised and federated learning addresses how learning methods can adapt across tasks and data settings. These topics are connected by a shared interest in representations: what information a method preserves, how that information supports learning, and whether a user can interpret the resulting structure.

3.1.2 Three linked directions

The first direction concerns representation and unsupervised learning, including nonnegative matrix factorization, clustering, dimensionality reduction, and meta-unsupervised learning. The second concerns learning from complex observations, including images, multimodal signals, and graph representations. The third concerns the conditions under which learning is useful in practice: limited labels, anomaly detection, medical interpretation, and privacy-preserving collaboration.

3.2. Research trajectory: from data representation to federated meta-unsupervised learning

My research has followed one question: how can a machine learn useful structures from data when labels are few and the data is spread across places that cannot share it? Each stage below leads to the next.

3.2.1 Representation and structure (2013–2021)

My early work studied how the way an image is represented affects what can be recovered from it: historical document restoration (Pattern Recognition, 2013; J36) and color-to-gray conversion (IEEE Transactions on Image Processing, 2015; J35). I also shared resources for other researchers (SMADI, LC2G, and the multispectral text extraction competition at ICDAR 2015). I then moved to non-negative matrix factorization (NMF). My paper, “NMF with feature relationship preservation penalty term for clustering problems” (Pattern Recognition, 2021; J20), shows that a representation must keep the structure of the data if it is to be useful for learning. This idea is the basis of my later work. This work won the IEEE Oman Section Best Paper Award (IEEE Region 8) in 2021. Appendix C3.

3.2.2 Learning with few labels, and on medical data

Next, I worked on learning when labelled examples are scarce. Examples are change detection in remote sensing (IEEE TGRS, 2016; J34, IEEE JSTARS, 2020; J26). I applied the same ideas to medical data with collaborators: histopathology images, Alzheimer’s-related modelling, sleep-stage analysis (J01, J08), seizure detection (C04, C01) and medical report summarization (C02). Two lessons from this work shaped my current program. Medical data has few labels. And it is sensitive, so it often cannot be pooled in one place.

3.2.3 Meta-unsupervised learning (2025)

I then asked how representation learning can work across many tasks instead of one. With my MSc student A. Khan, M. Allaoui and G. Zhong, I published “Meta-Unsupervised Representation Learning: A New Approach to Factorization and Interpretability” (Canadian AI, 2025; C10). It grew from a completed master’s project at Bishop’s. In parallel, my work on manifold learning and visualization (J10, J09, J07, J05) and on graph and anomaly methods (AISTATS 2026, C06; UAI 2026, C03) extends representation learning to embeddings, distributions and graphs.

3.2.4 Federated meta-unsupervised learning: the emerging program

My current program joins the two threads above: learning across tasks (meta-unsupervised learning) and learning across separate data holders without sharing their data (federated learning). The aim is privacy-preserving federated AI for healthcare, where data stays at each hospital or site. Early outputs are the preprint “FIRMA: FIbonacci Ring Model Aggregation for Privacy-preserving Federated Learning” (arXiv, 2026; PP01) and a submitted patent disclosure, Fed-Z-NMF (P01). They are not peer-reviewed publications or granted patents. The program is supported by my NSERC Discovery Development Grant, CFI / Mitacs / Research and Creative Activity Grants (Section 3.5), and it trains graduate students at Bishop’s (Appendix D).

I have shared my work as resources that others can use: the benchmark datasets SMADI and LC2G, the corrC2G algorithm (MathWorks File Exchange), and the MDSI image quality algorithm, which ranked first among 50 algorithms in an independent comparison (Appendix A9). Dated evidence of availability and use is in Appendix C3.

3.4.1 How funding supports the program

The NSERC and Bishop's awards support the development of a coherent research direction in autonomous and interpretable learning. The Mitacs project provides a setting in which AI methods can be investigated with an applied environmental objective. The CFI award concerns shared research infrastructure. My earlier SQU record includes PI support for matrix factorization and cancer detection, a project on intrusion detection, and co-investigator roles in work on medical records, machine-learning infrastructure, and analysis of student research projects; my postdoctoral and doctoral FRQNT awards are listed separately in Appendix A so that training support is not confused with faculty research funding.
