The next chapter in teaching, research and collaboration.

6. Future contribution

6.1 Priorities for the next stage

My next stage of work will deepen the connection between teaching, research, and student development. The priorities below are proposed directions; their scale will depend on student demand, available resources, approvals, and successful funding applications.

6.1.1 Teaching and student projects

I plan to develop a clearer progression from foundational computing and AI courses to supervised projects with a manageable scope, a clear question, appropriate data, and assessment that requires students to explain their reasoning. I also plan to strengthen my evidence of teaching effectiveness. I will document course revisions, review student feedback in context, and collect examples of assessed learning, including a documented response to the CS462/562 evaluation result noted in Section 2.2.2. I have developed the Natural Language Processing (CS320) and Large Language Models (CS520) courses I’m teaching for the first time in Fall 2026. I will revise them after their first offerings, using student feedback and assessment results if needed.

6.1.2 Research and supervision

My research priorities are to consolidate the meta-unsupervised learning program, develop work on representations and anomaly detection, and investigate privacy-preserving learning for distributed data. Medical and physiological data remain an important application direction through existing collaborations. For supervision, I plan to use clearly defined milestones, realistic project scope, and explicit contribution records, while seeking opportunities for students to present, publish, and collaborate.

6.1.3 Institutional contribution and collaboration

I intend to support interdisciplinary projects where computing expertise can help colleagues investigate a substantive question, and to help translate such problems into feasible student projects. Establishing BRAIN as a recognized research center, through a formal Senate proposal, is a near-term priority that would consolidate AI research capacity and graduate training at Bishop's.
