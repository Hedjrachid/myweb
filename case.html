Case for promotion

A connected record of academic contribution.

1. Case for promotion

1.1 Overview of academic contribution

My case rests on a connected record of academic contribution across three countries and eighteen years. I teach the foundations and methods students need to work with data; I develop research on how data can be represented and interpreted; and I use supervision and collaboration to bring students into that research. Service and academic leadership sustain the environment in which these activities take place. This application was brought forward in the current cycle at the Evaluation Committee's own invitation, in recognition of the continuity between my SQU and Bishop's records; Section 1.2 sets out that continuity and the review interval it implies.

1.1.1 Teaching and curriculum

My teaching has been recognized by the 2026 Excellence in Teaching Award at Bishop’s and by the Distinguished Academician (Best Teacher) award of the College of Science at Sultan Qaboos University (2020–2021); both certificates are in Appendix B4. My teaching record also includes developing CS219 (Python for CS and non-CS students) and CS446 (Intelligent Systems and Neural Networks) from scratch, and revising CS316 (Introduction to AI), CS503 (Data Visualization), CS307 (Databases), and CS504 (Programming Languages for Data Science). These contributions broaden access to computing for students outside the discipline while building a clearer path into advanced study for those within it. Supporting evidence is in Appendix B.

1.1.2 Research and scholarly development

My research is in artificial intelligence, mainly representation learning and unsupervised learning, with applications to healthcare, remote sensing and cybersecurity. I publish in highly ranked venues, including ACM Multimedia, UAI and AISTATS (A* and A ranks. Source CORE 2026) and Q1 with high factor impact journals such as IEEE Transaction in Image Processing, IEEE Transaction if Remote Sensing, Pattern Recognition and Expert Systems with Applications. Appendix C identifies the supporting evidence.

1.1.3 Research training and collaboration

My supervision spans several stages of research training across several institutions. At Bishop's, trainee topics include meta-unsupervised learning, seizure detection, medical report summarization, methane detection, and anomaly detection. My wider collaboration network, in Canada, Oman, Algeria, China, Italy, and Qatar, extends this training into joint work on medical data analysis and learning methods. Appendix D provides the full trainee roster.

1.1.4 Service and institutional contribution

My service combines Bishop's responsibilities with contributions to the wider discipline: Interim Chair, University committee work on teaching and generative AI, membership of the “Conseil scientifique de Calcul Québec”, scholarly assessment, editorial activity, and conference organization. Appendix E documents the work performed and its results.

1.1.5 The significance of the combined record

The contribution I bring to Bishop's is the ability to connect a broad teaching portfolio with an active research environment: courses introduce students to the subject, supervised projects give them a chance to investigate open questions, and collaborations extend the expertise and settings available for that work. My forward plan (Section 6) is to deepen these connections through carefully scoped research projects, stronger documentation of student learning, and sustained scholarly output.

1.2 Academic trajectory and review context

1.2.1 Development of an independent academic profile

My training in computer science and engineering established a foundation in clustering, image processing, and data representation. My master's research at the Université de Montréal examined graph-based Markovian clustering for image segmentation. My doctoral work at ÉTS (University of Quebec), completed with highest honor and funded by an FRQNT scholarship, addressed visual image processing in different representation spaces for documentary heritage preservation. A postdoctoral fellowship at McGill University (2013–2017), also FRQNT-funded, extended this work into machine learning, optimization, and multidimensional data analysis.

At Sultan Qaboos University, where I held faculty appointments from 2017 to 2023 — Assistant Professor (2017–2020) and then Associate Professor (2021–2023) — I built a teaching and research program in artificial intelligence, machine learning, and data science. My responsibilities there included graduate supervision, research funding as PI and co-PI, full curriculum development for a new MSc program, research group leadership (as chairperson of two research groups), and departmental committee service. This record is directly relevant background to the program I have continued at Bishop's, and is the basis for my request, under Article 7.06 g), that it be weighted equally with my Bishop's record.

1.2.2 Contributions since joining Bishop's

Since 2023, my work at Bishop's has combined teaching across several levels with the development of a research program in meta-unsupervised learning and applied AI. The record includes three Bishop's Research and Creative Activity Grants, an NSERC Discovery Development Grant, a Mitacs Accelerate award, and a co-PI role in a CFI John R. Evans Leaders Fund award. It also includes student co-authored publications, undergraduate research opportunities, curriculum development, and departmental service.

My appointment as Head of the AI Track at AMI-Lab (Research Centre on Aging, Université de Sherbrooke) began in January 2024. Adjunct appointments at UQO and the Université de Sherbrooke followed in 2026. These affiliations connect my work at Bishop's with complementary research and graduate-training environments and should be read alongside (not folded into) the specific supervision and collaboration records in Sections 3 and 4.
