4. Supervision and researcher development

4.1 A research training environment at several levels

My supervision includes postdoctoral, doctoral, master's, and undergraduate work. The value of this activity lies in developing researchers who can formulate a question, choose and evaluate methods, communicate findings, and work with others; I aim to make research training an active connection between teaching and scholarship. At Bishop's, my master's projects include meta-unsupervised learning with nonnegative matrix factorization and autoencoders, seizure detection using EEG and graph neural networks, medical report summarization, methane detection, lightweight AI, and anomaly detection in banking, giving students access to both methodological problems and applications with identifiable users.

4.2 Progression and student outcomes

Publication is not required of master’s students, but several of my master’s and doctoral students have published their work, and some have received awards.

At Bishop’s, my students received two Best Conference Student Paper Awards in 2025, one at the Canadian AI conference and one at IEEE BDDM. The award letters are linked in Appendix D2.

At Sultan Qaboos University (SQU), my students were also recognized:

An MSc student I supervised received the Best Supervised MSc Thesis award in 2020 and published the work at an international conference.

Two PhD students received the SQU Best Published Paper award, in 2023 and 2024 [confirm my role: supervisor or co-author].

A BSc student whose final-year project I supervised received a full scholarship for an MSc at Aalto University, after my recommendation. We later published a paper at ACM Multimedia, 2025. C08, Appendix A6.5.

At other institutions, I co-supervised doctoral and master’s students at Université de Sherbrooke, UQO, ESI Algeria, University of Ghardaïa, University of Ouargla and Hamad Bin Khalifa University. Degree completions and later positions are documented where confirmed.

4.3 Undergraduate and visiting research opportunities

Bishop's examples include a completed honors project on object detection and a completed USRA project on EEG preprocessing in 2024, and a completed Mitacs Globalink Research Internship in 2026 (BSc from ENIS Tunisia). Two further 2026 Mitacs Globalink Research Awards (a PhD and an MSc visitor from Algeria) are classified by their actual degree level as graduate visitors, rather than folded into undergraduate totals. See Appendix A10 for more information about undergrad and grad student supervision. See Appendix D2 for a sample of Mitacs ward notice.

4.4 Mentoring practice and attribution

A clear example of how my mentoring leads to research outcomes is the 2025 cohort of master’s students whose papers received Best Conference Student Paper Awards at the 38th Canadian AI Conference and at IEEE BDDM 2025 (Appendix D2). Bishop’s does not require MSc students to publish, but I encouraged them to, and I took on the extra supervision this needed. I helped each student treat the work as a question to defend before other researchers, not only as a deliverable to submit. I helped them turn it into a full paper, and guided them through submission, review and presentation. Both awards are the result of the students’ own work and presentation. My part was to guide the question, the writing and the submission.
